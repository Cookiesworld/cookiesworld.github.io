673 out of 700.

That's my AZ-204 score. Twenty-seven points short of a pass.

First reaction: frustration. So close, and it counts for nothing towards the certification itself.

Second reaction, a few days later: the score report is the most precise revision plan I've ever been handed. It told me exactly where I'm weak — user authentication and authorisation, Azure Functions, and message-based solutions.

There's a pattern in that list. They're the areas where my experience helps me least. Twenty-plus years of building things on .NET doesn't tell you which of four similar-looking Azure services Microsoft wants you to reach for in a given scenario, or why. The exam isn't testing whether I can build something — I can, and have, many times over. It's testing platform-specific judgement under exam conditions, with no IDE, no docs, and no chance to just try it and see.

A few concrete examples from the weak areas:

- **Managed Identity vs. Service Principal** — I know both, but the exam wants you to reflexively default to Managed Identity wherever it's viable, and I hadn't internalised that as the default reflex.
- **Durable Functions** — fan-out/fan-in vs. simple chaining patterns, and knowing which orchestration shape a scenario is actually describing.
- **Service Bus** — Topics with subscriptions, and dead-letter queue configuration via `MaxDeliveryCount`. Straightforward once you've used it in anger, easy to blur under exam pressure.

I went through the QA MAZ204 course properly back in May, and I'm booking the retake now with a targeted plan against those three areas rather than broad revision across the whole syllabus.

Failing a certification exam isn't the most comfortable thing to write about. But there's more value in documenting the real process than only posting the badge at the end — so consider this part one. Retake and retrospective to follow, either way it goes.
